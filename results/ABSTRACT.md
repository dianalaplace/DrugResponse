# Tumor Microenvironment Features Improve Prediction of Pathological Complete Response in Triple-Negative Breast Cancer and Immunotherapy Settings

## Abstract

**Background:** Pathological complete response (pCR) to neoadjuvant therapy is a key surrogate endpoint in breast cancer. While clinical and receptor-based features predict chemotherapy response in hormone receptor-positive (HR+) subtypes, their utility is limited in triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), where all patients share the same receptor phenotype. We hypothesized that tumor microenvironment (TME) features — including immune cell infiltration, immunosuppressive pathways, and stromal signatures — add independent predictive value for pCR in TNBC and immunotherapy settings.

**Methods:** We assembled two cohorts from public repositories: (1) a chemotherapy cohort of 476 patients with real neoadjuvant pCR endpoints from GEO (GSE25065, n=198; GSE20194, n=278), stratified into TNBC (n=137) and non-TNBC (n=339) subgroups; and (2) an immunotherapy cohort of 100 evaluable patients from the I-SPY2 trial (GSE173839; durvalumab + olaparib + paclitaxel). We derived 14 TME features from LM22 immune deconvolution signatures (111 marker genes) and trained XGBoost classifiers with 5-fold stratified cross-validation. Ablation studies compared clinical-only, clinical+TME, and TME-only models. SHAP analysis identified feature contributions. Cross-dataset validation (train on GSE25065, test on GSE20194 and vice versa) assessed generalizability.

**Results:** In the overall chemotherapy cohort, TME features did not improve prediction (AUC 0.749 clinical vs 0.746 clinical+TME; delta = -0.003). However, in the TNBC subgroup, TME added significant predictive value: AUC increased from 0.592 (clinical-only) to 0.641 (clinical+TME; delta = +0.049). SHAP analysis revealed that 9 of 10 top predictive features in TNBC were TME-derived, led by TGFb activity (SHAP = 0.545), MDSC infiltration (0.401), and M2 macrophage polarization (0.397). Cross-dataset validation confirmed generalizability (delta = +0.032 and +0.027 in held-out sets). In the immunotherapy cohort, TME features improved prediction from AUC 0.699 to 0.761 (delta = +0.062), with STAT1 signaling (0.458), T-cell infiltration (0.294), and PD1 expression (0.253) as top contributors. The multi-feature TME model (AUC = 0.738) outperformed single-biomarker PD-L1 (AUC = 0.689; delta = +0.049). TME did not add value in HR+ (delta = -0.046) or HER2+ (delta = -0.032) subgroups, where receptor status alone achieved AUC 0.695-0.750.

**Conclusions:** TME features provide independent predictive value for neoadjuvant pCR in two specific clinical contexts: TNBC chemotherapy, where conventional receptor-based features are uninformative, and checkpoint inhibitor immunotherapy, where pre-existing immune infiltration determines treatment efficacy. These findings support subtype-stratified integration of TME biomarkers into pCR prediction models and are consistent with prior evidence that TIL-dependent immune killing mediates chemotherapy response in TNBC (Denkert et al., Lancet Oncol 2018).

**Keywords:** tumor microenvironment, breast cancer, TNBC, pathological complete response, immunotherapy, SHAP, predictive biomarkers, neoadjuvant chemotherapy
